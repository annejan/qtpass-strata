DESCRIPTION
Twelve years of QtPass, the open-source GUI for pass, the standard Unix password manager, as a canyon wall.

Every layer of rock is one month of code. Left to right is time. A glowing front, "now", lays the rock down as it moves. Behind it, each layer is only as thick as the code from that month that still survives, so the old walls wear down as new work rewrites them. A faint high-water line shows where they once stood.

Sparks are commits, coloured by author. Beacons are releases. The red fault in the 2017 rock is QtPass's one CVE, CVE-2017-18021: passwords from the built-in generator were predictable until v1.2.1 sealed it in January 2018. The river of light is the translations, from 1 language to 64, with a tributary wherever new ones joined. The quiet years pass by night. Then 2026 erupts: 2,306 commits, 44% of all history, and 61% of the older code rewritten.

5,247 commits · 180 contributors · 64 languages · 72,701 lines of code, still cooling.
